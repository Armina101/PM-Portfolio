01

The problem

The problem was a habit I thought was mine.

Before Zuri, I thought the sums I did in my head before opening my banking app were just my habit. Then I asked friends, professionals and parents, and almost everyone did the same.

That quiet, everyday workaround was the real problem: people plan their money in their heads, in the language they think in, and only then open an app that wasn't built for that conversation.

02

What we built

Talk to your money, in the language you think in.

  • Four languagesEnglish, Pidgin, Yoruba and Hausa.
  • Plans with your real spendingAnswers are based on your actual transactions, not generic advice.
  • Voice to paymentA voice-to-payment flow with real account integration.
  • Nothing moves without youEvery payment waits for your confirmation and PIN.
03

Decisions and trade-offs

With money and voice together, trust had to come before speed.

  1. Local languages from the first build

    What I chose
    English, Pidgin, Yoruba and Hausa in the first version.
    Instead of
    Launching in English and adding languages later.
    What it cost
    More to design and test inside a one-week build.

  2. Confirmation and PIN before anything moves

    What I chose
    Every payment waits for an explicit confirmation and the user's PIN.
    Instead of
    Letting a voice command move money on its own.
    What it cost
    An extra step on every payment.

  3. Check every transaction independently

    What I chose
    A separate verification layer confirms each transaction.
    Instead of
    Trusting the assistant's own report of what happened.
    What it cost
    More to build in the same week.
