✎How I think

Principles I build by.

Four things I believe about building products, and the moments that taught me each one.

01

UsersLearned on Zuri

Look for the problem people have learned to live with.

Before Zuri, I thought calculating my balance before opening my banking app was just my habit. Then I spoke to friends, professionals and parents who did the same. What seemed personal was actually a shared problem.

See the Zuri project

02

ExperienceLearned on the hackathon platform

Design for everyone in the room.

On the hackathon platform, I designed for judges, participants and admins. Judges needed simple scoring, teams needed accessible private feedback, and admins needed a way to correct errors without changing a judge's comments.

See the hackathon project

03

OwnershipLearned on StacStart × iDICE

Give the team the problem, then get out of the way.

During the iDICE rebuild, I set clear goals and priorities, made progress visible in Jira, and trusted engineers to own their solutions. Everyone knew what mattered without needing constant oversight.

See the iDICE project

04

StrategyLearned on iDICE and Mimuads

Decide what not to build yet.

On iDICE, we prioritised bulk onboarding before job-readiness tools. At Mimuads, we validated venue-host interest before building the booking engine. Both taught me that knowing what to delay matters as much as knowing what to build.

See the iDICE and Mimuads projects

➜The journey

The plot so far

Some things make more sense with context. Pick a stop, or let it play.

  1. I learnt to question assumptions, follow the evidence, and understand complex problems. Outside the lab, I played volleyball for the university team, where I learned the value of teamwork, discipline, and showing up for the team.

  2. Built a foundation in software engineering, front-end development, and practical problem-solving. My projects, including my capstone, are available on GitHub ↗

  3. Started the university's first female-focused tech community and grew it to 350+ members.

  4. US-based software engineering fellowship where I strengthened my development skills through hands-on projects, building applications and practising technical problem-solving. The experience deepened my understanding of how software is built, tested, and improved.

  5. Grew membership 40% in a year to the largest GDG on Campus chapter in Sub-Saharan Africa, and co-led CareerFest X TechSummit for 2,600+ people.

  6. Co-founded with Femi Obadimu, we are building a self-serve marketplace for verified digital screen advertising. Now live in Lagos, the platform is growing as we work to simplify how businesses discover, book, and manage out-of-home advertising. So far, we’ve onboarded 50+ users across both sides of the marketplace, with 31 screen hosts and 20+ businesses listing their offerings on the platform. Read the case study.

  7. Led product development for Nodecademy at Splexxlabs, spanning the public-facing website, application tracking and payment workflows, and the Learning Management System (LMS). Translated business needs into Product Requirements Documents (PRDs) to guide engineering and align delivery across these connected experiences. The platform currently serves 50+ users and continues to scale.

  8. As a Product Manager at Conclase, I contribute to building and scaling StacStart, an AI-powered talent platform designed to bridge the gap between learning and employment. The platform brings together two complementary experiences: a Learning Management System (LMS) that supports learners in developing in-demand skills, and StacStart Jobs, which connects jobseekers with recruiters.

    One of my key contributions has been leading the rebuild of the StacStart LMS to support the iDICE Skills to Jobs programme, commissioned by the Bank of Industry, which targets 50,000+ learners across Northwest Nigeria, with StacStart as the implementation partner. Since inception, StacStart (formerly Conclase Academy) has also supported 10,000+ users across its learning and job application platform.

    My work spans translating business needs into product requirements, collaborating with engineering and cross-functional teams, and driving product delivery from planning through implementation. I also product-managed the platform for the Borderless Bytes Hackathon and transitioned the software development workflow from WhatsApp-based coordination to Jira, improving visibility and collaboration across the development lifecycle. In recognition of my contributions, I was named Staff of the Quarter for Q2 2026.
